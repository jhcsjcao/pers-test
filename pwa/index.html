About the Framework

What is OCEAN?

The Big Five (OCEAN) is the most widely validated model of personality in psychology. Discovered through decades of factor analysis across cultures, it describes five broad dimensions of temperament — not types, but spectrums. Everyone has all five traits to varying degrees.

  • Openness — curiosity, creativity, appetite for new ideas
  • Conscientiousness — discipline, order, drive to achieve
  • Extraversion — sociability, energy, outward engagement
  • Agreeableness — empathy, cooperation, harmony-seeking
  • Neuroticism — emotional sensitivity, stress reactivity

Public domain. No single researcher or company owns the Big Five — it is a scientific discovery, not a commercial product.

Unmet Childhood Needs

Research in developmental psychology identifies key domains where children need consistent, adequate support. When these needs are partially or fully unmet, adaptive coping patterns form — patterns that often persist into adulthood.

  • Attachment — safety, warmth, reliable emotional presence
  • Competence — autonomy, encouragement to master skills independently
  • Expression — validation of emotions, permission to feel openly
  • Play — unstructured joy, lightness, freedom from adult burdens
  • Limits — consistent boundaries, healthy structure and guidance

Drawn from attachment theory (Bowlby, Ainsworth), competence motivation (White, Bandura), emotion socialization research (Gottman), play therapy (Axline), and authoritative parenting literature (Baumrind).

How the Matrix Works

Your dominant OCEAN trait (your strongest temperament tendency) is crossed with your largest unmet childhood need to produce a behavioral archetype. Each archetype has a healthy expression (the adaptive strength you developed) and a shadow pattern (where the coping mechanism can limit you).

This is not a clinical diagnosis. It is a tool for self-reflection — understanding how your nature (temperament) and nurture (early environment) may have shaped who you are today.
